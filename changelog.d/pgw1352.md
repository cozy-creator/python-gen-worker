- **A re-roll takes another HOST, never another BUDGET.** Measured across twelve rentals on one
  evening: roughly half came up on a host whose driver cannot reach the fleet CUDA line
  (`550.127.05`, `610.43.02` on a workstation Ada part where forward-compat is unsupported), or
  never exposed a port inside the bring-up budget. A matrix that gives up on the first bad machine
  measures the provider's weather rather than the code, so `Rig.run(..., rerolls=N)` takes another
  host on a `reroll` verdict while `Rail.bank()` carries each dead pod's spend forward — `--rail
  2.00` means $2.00 however many machines the provider hands out, and the loop stops on MONEY rather
  than on a counter. Only `reroll` is a statement about the machine; `red`, `stuck` and `railed` are
  about the workload or the wallet and are not retried.
- **And the bring-up budget is PER POD, or every re-roll dies instantly.** Found the moment that
  loop first ran for real: row 2 re-rolled four times, each recording `reroll` at $0.000 and 0.0
  min, which reads as four consecutive dead hosts and was one arithmetic error. `check_sub` measured
  the per-POD bring-up allowance against `spent_usd()`, which now includes banked spend from earlier
  pods — with a $0.60 rail and a 10% sub-cap, the first bad host banked $0.065 and every later
  bring-up was over its allowance BEFORE its first observation. `pod_spent_usd()` splits the two
  questions: what has THIS pod cost (the allowance, which a re-roll resets) versus what has this
  INVOCATION cost (the rail, which must not).
