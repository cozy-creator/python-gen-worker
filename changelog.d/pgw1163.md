- **pgw#1163: the boot trace's child fan-out gets a DEVICE budget, so key derivation stops failing on the cards the fleet actually serves on.** `trace_workers` sized the pool from CPU alone — its docstring argued there was no VRAM bound because a structure-only export's allocator high-water is ~9.8 MiB (pgw#1080). That is true of the *tensors* and false of the *process*: a child that touches CUDA pays for a context plus the cuBLAS/cuDNN kernel images the export loads. Measured live on RTX 3090 `q1y1tx4fa1vjzs` (24 GB, sm_86), **uncontended** — fresh pod, one request, nothing else on the card: **7 children at 1.25-1.29 GiB each**, card at 3.12 MiB free of 23.56 GiB, all 18 declared sdxl classes refusing `torch.export … OUT OF DEVICE MEMORY`. The pod reported `host_cpu_count=96`, so K was capped only by the class count and 18 CUDA contexts landed on a 24 GB card. The identical derivation on a 48 GB A40 succeeded twice with a byte-identical key — which is why this read as "small cards cannot derive keys" instead of "the pool has no budget".

  **The fix bounds CONCURRENCY, not sharding.** How the classes are divided (`rows[i::K]`) is a correctness question and is untouched, so no key moves and nothing re-keys; how many children hold the card at once is a resource question, and `_run_children` now answers it from a measurement. The first child runs alone as a probe and reports `TraceReport.device_peak_bytes` — `total - free` on its own card, the whole process footprint, because the allocator's view misses the context that IS the cost. Every later wave is admitted against that number and the card's real free bytes, and the high-water only ever tightens.

  **No constant and no env.** An absent measurement keeps *exactly* the pre-pgw#1163 width — zero means "no evidence", never "no memory" — so this cannot throttle a pod nobody has measured. And it is not a serialization: `c9fb5d4a` is the cautionary case (the mint's entry pool ran K=1 fleet-wide for weeks, cost every mint 2.4x, and survived because nothing asserted the achieved width), so a card with room keeps its FULL width and a test asserts it. `PoolWidth.binding` names which bound decided, so a future collapse is assertable rather than inferable from prose.

  RED-proven in both directions: severing the bound fails 3 tests (the 3090 incident, the A4000 acceptance shape, the never-zero floor), and collapsing it to 1 fails the two anti-collapse assertions.

  **Not yet proven on a pod** — reaching one needs a published wheel, which is a coordinator decision. Related: th#1825 (sdxl's mint completes and then SIGSEGVs in `finalize` with 1.9 MB free of 47.7 GB, plausibly the same unbudgeted-fan-out shape one phase later).
