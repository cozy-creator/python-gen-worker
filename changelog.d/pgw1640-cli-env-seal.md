- **pgw#1640: `gen-worker up` serves under the platform's declared env, so the CLI and the pod no longer run different allocators.** `impose_process_env()` — which writes `settings_authority.DECLARED_ENV`, including `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` — ran at import of `gen_worker/entrypoint.py`, the POD entrypoint, and nowhere on the CLI's path. So a pod served with the declared allocator and `gen-worker up` (the local daemon, cozy-local's serve path) served with the stock one. pgw#1639 measured what that costs: at a 0.55 GiB enforced budget the same tree serves SDXL in ~40 s through the pod's env and returns a typed OOM refusal in ~6 s through the CLI's, out of 88.76 MiB of cache with no contiguous 40 MiB — exactly the fragmentation `expandable_segments` removes. The CLI package now imposes the declaration at import, before `..config` and long before anything that can reach torch, because the caching allocator reads that variable once at CUDA init and a later imposition is a silent no-op. `up --detach` also folds the declaration into the child's environment at exec, so the seal is visible in `/proc/<pid>/environ` rather than only `putenv`-ed after the fact, and the daemon verifies its own `os.environ` against the declaration before booting — refusing the boot when it does not bind — and confesses the effective values on its boot line and in its handle document. The env half only: `env_seal.establish()` stays the pod's, because its `verify_interpreter_env()` cannot pass in a console-script process and its remedy re-execs `sys.orig_argv`, which is correct for `python -m gen_worker.entrypoint` and wrong for `…/bin/gen-worker`.
- **Every local floor/memory measurement inherits the seal.** A local benchmark taken through `gen-worker up` before this understated the floor by a fragmentation term; pgw#1602's floor rig and any future local rig must now either boot through this path or confess on every row that they did not.
